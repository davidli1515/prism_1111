Review of the minimal pilot's interpretation (no additional model queries)

The arithmetic and data separation checks passed, but that does not establish
that the experimental design could detect incremental MSCS information.
The result supports only: this particular ridge specification did not improve
predictions for these three mcf sets. It does not justify rejecting attainable-
gain modeling, resource-conditioned MSCS bounds, or MSCS guidance generally.

Additional checks of saved data:
  - Ten gcc training roots all have CPI in [4.920690, 5.132464]. Test mcf roots
    have CPI [2.281745, 2.250736, 2.164469]. Training on one workload conflates
    informational value with cross-workload extrapolation.
  - Four training roots form one component connected by single non-FE parameter
    changes. The ten rows are not ten independent microarchitectural contexts;
    this relation yields seven components within the gcc training set.
  - All three held-out regions are exactly the same resource pattern:
    fetchBufferSize already 64, l1i_assoc 4 -> {8,16,32}. They provide no test of
    joint fetch-buffer/associativity effects or diverse remaining-resource ranges.
  - Two test targets are zero. The third is only 0.0924837% relative CPI gain.
  - Scalar has 229 features, MSCS has 829, with ten training rows and fixed ridge
    alpha=1. This choice was not validated for signal detection on training data.
  - Training R2 is 0.317625 for scalar and 0.317858 for MSCS. Fitted training gain
    spans only roughly 0.462%..0.690%, versus actual 0.233%..1.518%.
  - Effective ridge degrees of freedom (excluding intercept) are 0.491820 and
    0.493383. Adding MSCS barely changes the fitted model's effective flexibility.
  - After the fixed block scaling, summed training variance is 0.0344524 for
    architecture, 0.0261796 for region ranges, 0.000137544 for CPI distributions,
    and 0.000199254 for MSCS composition. Input scaling and limited training
    diversity sharply restrict what the extra features can contribute.
  - Mean-gain baseline test MAE is 0.535852 percentage points, versus 0.483936
    for scalar and 0.484560 for MSCS. Predictions are all near the training mean.

Revised interpretation:
This is an inconclusive signal-detection pilot, with no positive evidence for
this implementation. Its failure cannot distinguish insufficient MSCS signal
from an underinformative dataset, regularization/representation choices, or
workload shift. The earlier report's recommendation not to expand this exact
implementation remains practical; it must not be read as evidence against the
research direction. Correct code execution is separate from a diagnostic design.

Before another substantive conclusion: check low-dimensional, physically defined
features and train-only preprocessing/regularization; include a positive control
where added MSCS contains a known learnable signal; use grouped validation and
preselected held-out regions with different remaining-resource patterns. These
three mcf cases have now been examined and must not become a newly tuned method's
claimed unseen confirmation set. No follow-up experiment was launched in this review.
