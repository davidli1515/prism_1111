Background: hpca2027-paper1968.pdf, Section V (masked-value Protect), and
dse_v2/mscs_1008.md. This experiment tests conditional branch ordering, not a
new resource-headroom representation, a change to the model, or a proof that
pressure magnitude measures resource value.

Fixed before full runs:
  Six workloads: gcc, mcf, xalancbmk, imagick, leela, exchange2.
  Seeds: 42, 43, 44. Three methods per workload/seed.
  One fresh 500-query BO initialization shared by the three methods.
  Each method spends exactly 5000 distinct surrogate configuration queries.
  Same checkpoint, normalization and lexicographically selected 50 even slices.
  Reference = 1.1 times the componentwise maximum of the common initial BO
  archive, fixed before refinement. Comparisons across seeds use paired ratios.

Immediate scalar baseline:
  Evaluate every +1 direction, choose positive CPI gain / incremental area.
  From that intermediate state evaluate -1/-4 reclaims on other parameters;
  accept cost <= 0.3% and choose greatest area saving.

Guarded methods (identical except ranking):
  Among cheap, nonnegative-cost reclaims at fixed L1 trace size, rank by either
  per-slice relative CPI-cost P90 (scalar_guard) or mean per-slice L1 change of
  FP/BP/SP proportions conditional on non-Base mass (mscs_guard).
  Select at most three distinct reclaim parameters (one stride per resource).
  Evaluate +1/+4/+8 future probes, excluding L1-size changes; select the best
  observed improvement for each of the top three distinct scalar directions.
  Charge ALL these precursor/reference queries. Then query at most nine joint
  configurations in ranked reclaim order. Require actual configuration effects
  to commute and add, excluding projection artifacts.
  Only a measured joint whose CPI cost versus its own future reference is
  <= 0.3% and whose area is smaller is a verified reclaim for that future.
  Choose the next actual configuration among future references, verified
  joints and directly CPI-improving reclaims, using the common gain/area rule.
  If none improves the intermediate state, retain the immediate scalar choice.
  A bad joint does not ban that resource on other paths. Unqueried joints are
  not implicitly safe. Every queried valid-area configuration enters the archive,
  including risky/rejected joints and probes not chosen for the walk.

Shared mechanics:
  Maximum 15 steps per walk; previously expanded states are not expanded again.
  When the Pareto frontier is exhausted, take 30 charged random restart probes
  using a fixed method-independent seed, until the exact budget is spent.
  Stopping at a partial step still retains all observations in the archive.
  Physical inference/area reuse across methods is allowed; a method must still
  pay for every configuration on its first observation. No cross-arm decisions
  or labels are accessible before that payment.
  Private McPAT XML directories; independent SQLite cache keyed by exactly the
  XML-relevant settings and salted with model/template/executable hashes.
  Area failures are excluded from the Pareto set and logged. Their surrogate
  observations still consume budget. No legacy area cache or frontier is used.

Primary question:
  At 5000 total queries, is MSCS ordering better than scalar_guard ordering?
  Compare paired HV, HV curves, query allocation and actual joint steps taken.
  Scalar baseline is secondary: it differs in the whole lookahead mechanism.
  Risk detection alone is not success. More detected risks without more HV
  is a negative end-to-end result for this implementation.
  Per-arm wall time is NOT a speed comparison: later arms reuse physical caches.
  This run measures query efficiency. A wall-time/Jacobian claim requires a
  separate equally warmed or cold-cache timing experiment.

Limits:
  Surrogate-only results cannot establish gem5 Pareto quality or generalization.
  Full-budget random and BO are required before claiming superiority to those
  algorithms (especially given the negative greedy results in mscs_1008.md).
  The present experiment does not evaluate Jacobian acceleration. J_CPI is a
  scalar baseline; J_MSCS would need its own matched-budget ablation. Existing
  arch-slot-only derivatives omit configuration-dependent distribution inputs.
